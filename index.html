How to read this

Your entries fail when you buy hope inside chop. Every alert here encodes the same rule: compression tells you a move is loading, volume tells you the move is real, and only the combination is an entry. No signal, no trade — let the tool watch the chart so you don't.

Squeeze (the "about to move" indicator)

The middle panel is a TTM-style squeeze. Consolidation is volatility compression: Bollinger Bands (2σ) shrink inside Keltner Channels (1.5 ATR).

  • Red dots on the zero line = squeeze ON. Energy is loading. Do not enter here — this is exactly where theta eats your premium while price goes nowhere.
  • Green dots = squeeze released. The first green dot after ≥5 red dots is the classic trigger.
  • The histogram gives direction: above zero and brightening = up momentum, below zero and brightening = down.

This is the answer to "when will price actually move": you can't know when, but the squeeze tells you the market is coiled, so you wait for the release instead of guessing inside the coil.

RVOL (the fakeout filter)

Relative volume = this bar's volume ÷ its 20-bar average. It is the single best fakeout filter.

  • Breakout on RVOL < 1 → probably a fakeout. Skip it.
  • Breakout on RVOL ≥ 1.5–2 → real participation. Bright volume bars in the panel mark these.
  • Volume should lead or confirm the break, not show up two bars late.

"When I enter with momentum I'm usually right" — RVOL is how you quantify momentum instead of feeling it, so you can also see when it's absent.

Volume profile (where price can run)

The histogram on the right edge of the chart shows how much volume traded at each price over the visible window.

  • POC (orange line) = the fairest price. Price gets magnetized back to it in chop.
  • Value area (shaded, 70% of volume): inside it = balance = expect rotation, not trends.
  • Thin zones (short bars) = low-volume nodes. Price moves fast through them — a break into a thin zone has room to run; a break into a thick node will stall.

Before entering, check what's above/below: squeeze release + thin profile ahead = the good trade. Release straight into a fat node = take profits early or skip.

VWAP + Stoch RSI (your tools, with rules)

Keep using them — but as filters, not triggers.

  • VWAP (orange curve) = the session's average price. Longs above, shorts below; a reclaim/loss on RVOL is a trend signal, a cross on dead volume is noise.
  • Stoch RSI (bottom panel) times entries within a trend: in an uptrend, buy the %K hook up from <20; don't short "overbought" in a strong trend — it stays pinned.

Stoch RSI alone can't tell consolidation from trend — that's why it's been trapping you. Squeeze + RVOL decide if; Stoch RSI and VWAP decide where.

The checklist (this is the actual edge)

Only enter when a closed bar gives you all of:

  • Squeeze just released (or clean level break) — not mid-chop.
  • RVOL ≥ 1.5 in your direction.
  • Price on the right side of VWAP.
  • Thin volume profile ahead, fat node behind (your stop hides behind it).

If you're buying options: entering during a squeeze = paying theta to wait. Entering on the release = the move pays for the premium. The waiting problem isn't discipline, it's having no objective trigger — now the trigger pings you.
