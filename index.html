How to read this

Your entries fail when you buy hope inside chop. Every alert here encodes the same rule: compression tells you a move is loading, volume tells you the move is real, and only the combination is an entry. No signal, no trade — let the tool watch the chart so you don't.

Why no price target or ETA (and what you get instead)

This tool used to project: a lean score, "similar setups moved $X" percentages, a dotted path. Its own backtest measured all of it as a coin flip — strong signals scored worse than modest ones — so it was deleted. Any read built from completed bars describes what has already started; dressing that as a forecast is how tools earn false trust.

What you get instead is measured: on a MOVING read, the How much is left card shows this move's age and travel against the distribution of every completed run on this same chart, plus the distance to the next tested level at the move's current pace. History and arithmetic, each labeled as what it is.

The practical rule it supports: enter moves while they're fresh (the backtest's fresh-vs-late rows measure the chase penalty on your own chart), and take targets from structure, not from hope.

Squeeze (the "about to move" indicator)

The middle panel is a TTM-style squeeze. Consolidation is volatility compression: Bollinger Bands (2σ) shrink inside Keltner Channels (1.5 ATR).

  • Red dots on the zero line = squeeze ON. Energy is loading. Do not enter here — this is exactly where theta eats your premium while price goes nowhere.
  • Green dots = squeeze released. The first green dot after ≥5 red dots is the classic trigger.
  • The histogram gives direction: above zero and brightening = up momentum, below zero and brightening = down.

This is the answer to "when will price actually move": you can't know when, but the squeeze tells you the market is coiled, so you wait for the release instead of guessing inside the coil.

RVOL (the fakeout filter)

Relative volume = this bar's volume ÷ its 20-bar average. It is the single best fakeout filter.

  • Breakout on RVOL < 1 → probably a fakeout. Skip it.
  • Breakout on RVOL ≥ 1.5–2 → real participation. Bright volume bars in the panel mark these.
  • Volume should lead or confirm the break, not show up two bars late.

"When I enter with momentum I'm usually right" — RVOL is how you quantify momentum instead of feeling it, so you can also see when it's absent.

Volume profile (where price can run)

The histogram on the right edge of the chart shows how much volume traded at each price over the visible window.

  • POC (orange line) = the fairest price. Price gets magnetized back to it in chop.
  • Value area (shaded, 70% of volume): inside it = balance = expect rotation, not trends.
  • Thin zones (short bars) = low-volume nodes. Price moves fast through them — a break into a thin zone has room to run; a break into a thick node will stall.

Before entering, check what's above/below: squeeze release + thin profile ahead = the good trade. Release straight into a fat node = take profits early or skip.

VWAP + Stoch RSI (your tools, with rules)

Keep using them — but as filters, not triggers.

  • VWAP (orange curve) = the session's average price. Longs above, shorts below; a reclaim/loss on RVOL is a trend signal, a cross on dead volume is noise.
  • Stoch RSI (bottom panel) times entries within a trend: in an uptrend, buy the %K hook up from <20; don't short "overbought" in a strong trend — it stays pinned.

Stoch RSI alone can't tell consolidation from trend — that's why it's been trapping you. Squeeze + RVOL decide if; Stoch RSI and VWAP decide where.

ATR — the size of a normal move

Average True Range = the average size of one bar, in dollars. That's all it is. It matters because it converts everything into "is this normal?":

  • Stops: a stop closer than ~1 ATR is inside random noise — you'll be stopped out by nothing. Put it beyond 1 ATR and behind a level.
  • Targets: expecting a 4-ATR move by end of day is a lottery ticket; 1–2 ATR is a normal expectation.
  • Regime: a bar suddenly 1.5× its recent average range = the market woke up (that's the range-expansion alert).

You don't compute it — the tool does. You just need the idea: measure moves in ATRs, not in hopes.

FVG — fair value gaps (the shaded boxes)

A 3-bar imbalance: price moved so fast that bar 1's range never overlaps bar 3's — the band in between traded almost no volume. The chart shows unfilled ones as shaded boxes.

  • Price often comes back to "fill" the gap — a green (bullish) box below price is a magnet-then-support zone; a red box above is a magnet-then-resistance zone.
  • Best use: entries. Instead of chasing the breakout candle, wait for the pullback into the gap it left, with a stop below the box.
  • A gap that keeps rejecting price = strong; a gap sliced through without pause = the move behind it is dead.

FVGs and volume profile agree with each other: both mark where almost nothing traded — and price moves fast through those zones and reverses at their edges.

RSI: which days it works (and which it wrecks you)

"Overbought" does not mean "about to fall." In a real trend RSI pins above 80 for hours — fading it is how RSI loses you money. The rule is regime first:

  • Chop day (price crossing VWAP over and over, going nowhere): RSI extremes work — fade 80 back toward VWAP/POC, buy 20.
  • Trend day (one side of VWAP all day, efficient movement): do NOT fade. Use RSI dips to ~40–50 as pullback entries in the trend direction.
  • Timeframes: the higher timeframe is the dog, yours is the tail. 45m pointing down + 5m bouncing = a bounce to short into, not a reversal. Trade the small frame only in the big frame's direction.

That's why some days "trading purely off RSI" works and other days it doesn't: it was never RSI, it was the day type.

Volume profile, the 60-second course

The sideways histogram = how much traded at each price (not each time). Three things to read off it:

  • POC (orange dashes) — the single price with the most volume. It's where buyers and sellers agreed most, so in quiet conditions price gravitates back to it. Far from POC = stretched; at POC = fair, no edge.
  • Value area (the darker 70% band) — "accepted" prices. Inside it: rotation, fade edges back to POC. Price escaping value on volume = a real repricing — that's a trend starting, don't fade it.
  • Thin shelves (short bars) — prices nobody accepted. Price slips through them fast (good for targets beyond them) and finds nothing to stand on (bad places for stops).

Practical loop: before entering, ask "where's the next thick node?" — that's your realistic target — and "is a thin shelf in the way?" — that's your acceleration zone. After a big move up, price consolidating ON TOP of a fat node it just built = healthy flag; consolidating on thin air = prone to slipping back.

The checklist (this is the actual edge)

Only enter when a closed bar gives you all of:

  • Squeeze just released (or clean level break) — not mid-chop.
  • RVOL ≥ 1.5 in your direction.
  • Price on the right side of VWAP.
  • Thin volume profile ahead, fat node behind (your stop hides behind it).

If you're buying options: entering during a squeeze = paying theta to wait. Entering on the release = the move pays for the premium. The waiting problem isn't discipline, it's having no objective trigger — now the trigger pings you.
